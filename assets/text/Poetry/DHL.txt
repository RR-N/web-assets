I love the riverbeds in the latest days of the summer months, when leaves turn shades but rest easy on their mother canopies – when golden rays drown the woods in auburn warmth and the taut skins of the current refract the lights into a bright and shining sprite atop the water clear. My heaven, should it exist, must look like these birch woods, frozen in their golden hour, alive forever between the summer heat and the falling leaves.

I love the low mountains draped in deep sheets of light snow, when the crust of glittering frost sits young beneath the sun, and breaks under my step like the crust of fresh bread. My heaven, should it exist, must look like New England in the winter, the forest undisturbed and deep aslumber in the cold coma of the snow, unperturbed and safe but for the steps I draw on its white canvas.

I love the buzz and the rings, and the noises of animalia, awash about the canopies of less lonely evergreens drinking in the spoils of late springs – when little life awakens and the elder furs resume their march to motion, and when the stillness of long nights cedes fields like theater unto the cacophony of souls that drum their hearts to beats rabid and symphonious. My heaven, should it exist, must surely look like this; the sawing signs of beings beyond mine and their undefeated, still-yet-breathing, encumbrance – with the majors, minors, hashes, and lashes, of the painterly natures of life.
